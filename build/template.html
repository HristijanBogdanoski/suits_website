The collection

Presented as the cloth book, the way it is on the cutting table — the bolt first, the garment second.

In the atelier

Six fittings, eleven weeks, one pattern that stays on file for the rest of your life.

Cut once, for one person.

Your pattern is drawn from twenty-two measurements and a standing posture, then cut in full canvas and basted for the first fitting.

Nothing is fused. Nothing is bought in.

Notes on this prototype

Built to be argued with. Nothing here is precious.

What's working

  • The scroll lock. The page freezes and your wheel drives the reveal, then it releases the moment the sequence finishes.
  • The release valves. It re-engages if you scroll back up, never engages for reduced-motion, and Escape, Tab or Skip always gets you out. The component this came from had none of these — it trapped the page permanently.
  • Everything is free. Photography is Pexels stock, licensed for commercial use with no attribution. Nothing here has been paid for.

What's placeholder

  • The photographs are stock. They set the register; they are not your garments.
  • Names, cloths and prices are invented and live in one list.
  • The cloth book is drawn, not shot — each weave is CSS, which is why the six are perfectly consistent. Real bolt photography would replace them.

What I'd do next

  • Decide on the hero. This reveal, or the generated shot of the tuxedo dropping onto the form. Roughly $19 buys the second.
  • Open the cards. A detail view with the full spec and a fitting request.
  • Give me one reference. Any site whose feel you want — I'll take the mechanism from it, the way I took the scroll lock from the one you sent.
